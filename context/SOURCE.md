## What it is
Canvas LMS enhancement / engagement layer for virtual and online K-12 schools. Turns an existing Canvas course into a visual, motivating student experience and an early-warning system for teachers in about three minutes — no migration, no extra teacher work: they just turn it on. Not a feature-vs-feature SIS/LMS rival. Not an LMS replacement. Not a website.

## Positioning statement (from pack)
For virtual and online K-12 schools running courses in Canvas, Delphinium is the Canvas engagement layer that turns an existing Canvas course into a visual, motivating experience for students and an early-warning system for teachers in about three minutes — unlike general LMS tools, which deliver content but don't address whether students want to engage with it.

## 25-word description
Delphinium turns the Canvas courses virtual schools already have into engaging experiences for students and early-warning systems for teachers — with no extra work for teachers. Just turn it on.

## Category claim
Lives in the gap between content delivery and student motivation. Founder: tools for content are plentiful; tools that help students *want* to engage are not — that’s what Delphinium was designed to do.

## Proof (do not invent beyond this)
- **Davis Connect / Davis School District (Utah):** 72 fully online classes, 6,000 students; same courses, same content, same teachers; year without → year with; asynchronous (students never met teacher/classmates in person). **Result: 31% decrease in course failure rates.**
- **31% framing (Jared 2026-09-04):** Always use **“up to 31%”** when making a promise / forward-looking claim (ads, homepage, proposals, pricing, CTAs). Use exact **31%** only when citing the Davis study by name with study context (72 classes / 6,000 students / same courses·teachers·content).
- **72%** of students: Delphinium is more / much more motivating than a traditional Canvas course. Most common open-ended word: **“Fun.”**
- Student survey: *“I was sitting on the couch watching Netflix and the thought popped into my head: I could be doing homework right now.”*
- **Natalie Niederhauser** (HS Math, Davis Connect, 100+ students): ~12 emails/day → ~6 manual messages/week; *“saved most of my Saturday nights.”*
- **Tiffany Dance** (Instructional Coach, Davis Connect): game changer; students redoing quizzes for avatar points / googly eyes.

## Pain → answer (★ reuse)
Gray wall of text / Canvas boring → 3-minute Makeover. Low engagement / not logging in → gamification + nudges for the majority (progress/belonging, not only competition). High failure/withdrawal → Control Tower + Davis proof. Parent “I don’t know, Dad” → color-coded parent view. Teacher outreach buried → Message Center (~40 seconds to message struggling group). Messages not reaching students → multi-channel send. Quiet “suffering silence” failers → rule-based outreach. Inconsistent Canvas → school-wide templates. No migration / no new tool to learn / Chromebooks / FERPA — see synthesis objections.

## Behavioral foundation (qualitative)
Self-regulated learning · behavioral economics / nudges · social presence. Competition-vs-belonging: most students aren’t primarily competition-motivated; design for progress, belonging, goal-tracking (keep qualitative on public pages; message-map’s 6%/94% is internal until Jared OKs a public %).

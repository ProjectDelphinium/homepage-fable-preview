**Purpose:** Inform a premium but personal / fun / quirky / approachable / cutting-edge redesign of Delphinium (delphi-me.com). Research patterns — not pixel-for-pixel competitor copying.  
**Buyer focus:** Directors of Digital / Online Learning, virtual academy leaders; IT as LTI/risk gate.

## Sources & method (high level)

Web research (2024–2026) across: K-12 marketing / GEO visibility reports; institutional buyer trust frameworks (Katalysts, Insivia, Midday Advisors, Winsome); Instructure Edtech Top 40 / LTI ecosystem signals; Canvas-partner marketing patterns (Classcraft, Nearpod, Packback); authentic photography vs stock in school branding; K-12 vs Higher Ed pitch differences. ## Market context that shapes design (2025–2026)

1. **Evidence over access.** Districts have access to thousands of tools but students/educators actively use ~4 LTI tools on average (Instructure 2026 Edtech Top 40). Buyers are consolidating, not collecting. Sites that look like “another shiny add-on” lose; sites that prove *worth keeping in the Canvas stack* win.
2. **~3-minute trust filter.** Institutional buyers spend roughly three minutes on vendor materials before deciding whether to engage. Named implementations, measurable outcomes, LMS/SIS fit, and privacy posture must surface immediately — not after a feature tour.
3. **Product-first openings fail.** District buyers read “here’s our product” as “you don’t understand our constraints.” Lead with their Monday-morning problem (online motivation, Day-180 engagement, chasing non-starters) then bridge to Delphinium.
4. **LTI is table stakes for IT.** Seamless Canvas launch, SSO, grade/roster reality, “works inside what we already own” — not rip-and-replace. Marketing that treats integration as a footnote triggers the gate.
5. **Authenticity is scarce → competitive.** Buzzwords, stock faces, and AI-smooth copy create a credibility crisis. Specific, educator-voiced, evidence-backed communication stands out *because* it’s rare.
6. **Discovery is AI-influenced, but the website remains the destination.** Answer-first clarity, named proof, and scannable structure help both human scanners and citation-style discovery — still convert on the site.

## Responds well to (concrete patterns) — 8

### 1) Story shape: Hook → Problem → Insight → Bridge → Product → Proof → Takeaway → CTA
Matches how DOLs evaluate risk. Open on a recognizable online-program pain (teachers chasing students who haven’t started; parents learning late; motivation at Day 180), not a feature grid. Insight (“more reports ≠ student work”) earns the right to introduce Delphinium as the bridge that turns Canvas engagement data into action. Aligns with existing marketing desk story arc.

### 2) Proof above the fold — named, quantified, methodology-visible
Place **Davis / 31% course-failure drop / 72 classes / 6,000+ students / same courses·teachers·content** and **72% more motivating** where a 3-minute scanner cannot miss them. Named people (Natalie Niederhauser, Tiffany Dance + role) beat anonymized “district partner” blurbs. Show *context* (fully online classes, comparable conditions) so the champion can defend the number internally. Prefer one deep case over a logo wall.

### 3) Tone: peer, specific, lightly human — not “enterprise SaaS” or “revolutionize learning”
Language that sounds like a conversation with another digital-learning director: concrete verbs (identify → understand → communicate → intervene → engage), no superlatives (“game-changing,” “best-in-class”). Quirky/personal works *if* it signals a real team (Jared / Delphinium personality) rather than a mascot pasted on generic B2B. Fun can live in motion, copy asides, and UI personality — while outcome claims stay sober and sourced.

### 4) Imagery: real product UI + real educator moments > stock / AI faces
Buyers spot stock and AI-generated faces in seconds; they read as “couldn’t show your own work.” Prefer: authentic Canvas+Delphinium screens (engagement → action), candid virtual/hybrid classroom or facilitator moments, real people tied to named proof. Imperfect candid > glossy staged. Motion that *demonstrates* the product (scroll-scrubbed UI walkthrough) outperforms hero video of smiling stock teens.

### 5) Motion with a job: clarify the narrative, don’t decorate
Scroll-triggered story beats, pinned “problem → action” sequences, subtle UI reveals that show *how* engagement becomes intervention. Restraint: motion that distracts or slows Lighthouse kills trust with IT-minded buyers. Premium + cutting-edge = purposeful micro-interaction, not confetti on every section.

### 6) Density: scannable layers, not wall-of-features or sparse “agency emptiness”
Committee buyers need ammunition fast: outcome headline → one proof block → one integration reassurance (LTI / works with Canvas) → CTA. Secondary depth (implementation notes, IT FAQ, longer case) one click away. Avoid both (a) CMS feature-grid templates and (b) ultra-minimal sites that hide substance. Dual paths: DOL story path + IT/LTI path without splitting the homepage into a K12-vs-HE chooser.

### 7) “Maximize Canvas you already own” framing (ecosystem ally, not rival)
Canvas-partner patterns that land: Nearpod/Classcraft-style “embed in the workflow / no multi-tab teaching / save teacher time.” Position Delphinium as engagement enhancement *inside* Canvas — post-ESSER value from existing investment. Never attack Canvas; never imply rip-and-replace. Visual language can show Canvas chrome + Delphinium layer so IT sees coexistence.

### 8) Champion ammunition built into the page
Procurement mindset: “Can I defend this to my principal / board / CTO?” Surface downloadable or copyable proof (named case, methodology note, LTI summary, realistic implementation expectations). Transparency about what Delphinium does *not* do (within approved claims) increases trust. Pricing honesty where possible; if not public, clear next-step without dark patterns.

## Turns them off (concrete patterns) — 8

### 1) Stock photography, AI-generated faces, and “diverse classroom” templates
Instantly read as inauthentic. Especially toxic next to claims about student motivation/engagement.

### 2) Fabricated or anonymized social proof; carousel of vague testimonials
“Trusted by leading institutions,” star ratings without narrative, role-only quotes with no context, AI-written praise. Buyers want named peers with parallel responsibility (virtual-school / DOL roles). **Hard no for Delphinium:** invent customers or strengthen unapproved claims.

### 3) Buzzword fog and transformation hype
“Revolutionize,” “personalized learning journeys,” “21st-century,” “AI-powered insights” without a Monday-morning sentence. Triggers eye-roll and filters the vendor as product-first / reality-distant.

### 4) Feature grids and CMS template tells
Icon rows of capabilities with no story, Webflow/WordPress theme sameness, generic SaaS purple gradients. Signals commodity tool in a market consolidating away from tool sprawl.

### 5) Attacking or diminishing Canvas (or any LMS)
IT and Canvas admins are gates. Competitive LMS wars kill deals. Enhancement language only.

### 6) Product-first hero with no district/online context
“Welcome to Delphinium — the engagement platform” before naming who it’s for and what hurt it removes. Same for HE-academic tone on the homepage (HE is secondary path only).

### 7) Hiding integration, privacy, and implementation reality
LTI buried in footer; “seamless” with no how; no timeline honesty. IT fails the vendor in the invisible audit. Also: compliance theater (fake badges, invented certifications).

### 8) Inbox-energy marketing on the site
Spammy urgency, fake countdown scarcity, identical nurture-feeling CTAs, over-produced “transformative experiences” copy. Superintendents/DOLs are drowning in AI-spun pitches; the site must feel like the opposite: calm, specific, human.

## Optional contrast: Higher Ed LMS add-on marketing tone

| | **K-12 online / virtual (primary ICP)** | **Higher Ed LMS add-on (secondary)** |
|---|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Lead with** | Operational pain, motivation, Day-180 engagement, teacher workload, board-defensible outcomes | Pedagogical flexibility, research framing, faculty buy-in, student success / retention language |
| **Decision shape** | More top-down / district or academy leadership + IT gate; fiscal calendar | Often bottom-up faculty pilot → committee; longer iterative cycle |
| **Proof style** | Comparable *district / virtual academy* outcomes, ESSA-adjacent evidence mindset, usage consolidation | Peer-institution case studies, faculty quotes, academic integrity / efficacy nuance |
| **Tone risk** | Corporate SaaS or HE-jargon on homepage alienates DOLs | K-12 “gamify the classroom” energy can feel unserious to faculty |
| **Site implication for Delphinium** | Homepage = K-12 online story + proof; HE as secondary path / page, not hero chooser | Keep HE tone available without owning first paint |

## Non-website reference vibes (research suggestions — not Jared’s words)

Labeled as **design-research mood references** only; useful for art direction conversations, not copy claims.

1. **Film / TV — *Abbott Elementary* (mockumentary warmth)**  
   Professional competence + humor + care for kids without cynicism or corporate gloss. Tone: adults who know the job is hard and still show up. Maps to personal / approachable / lightly quirky without clownishness.

2. **Games — *Nintendo Switch* UX / *Animal Crossing* clarity with delight**  
   Friendly, inviting UI personality; systems that feel playful but never obscure the task. Cutting-edge here means *delight in service of clarity* — good metaphor for engagement UX that teachers won’t fight. Avoid chaotic “gamification carnival” (points everywhere, no pedagogy).

3. **Magazine / editorial — *The Pudding* + *Kinfolk*-adjacent restraint (hybrid)**  
   *The Pudding*: scrollytelling that makes data human and visual without marketing fluff. Kinfolk-ish: sparse, personal photography, breathing room. Hybrid = evidence you can *feel*, not a whitepaper PDF dump or a loud SaaS landing template.

*(Alternate if needed: Pixar short-film character warmth for illustration system; Duolingo’s personality only as a cautionary upper bound — too mascot-forward for DOL buyers unless heavily dialed down.)*

## Design implications checklist for delphi-me.com redesign

- [ ] Homepage default story = K-12 online DOL; HE secondary; no chooser as hero.
- [ ] First screen: human online-learning problem + one hard proof number (31% / 72%) + CTA to schedule.
- [ ] Named proof (Davis context; Natalie / Tiffany per SOURCE) early; methodology visible.
- [ ] Canvas = ally language + LTI path for IT within 1–2 scrolls or clear nav.
- [ ] Real product UI imagery / motion demo; zero AI faces / stock classroom clichés.
- [ ] Quirky-personal voice in microcopy & motion; claims stay evidence-tight.
- [ ] Avoid feature-grid CMS look; avoid attacking Canvas; avoid fabricated testimonials.
- [ ] Champion pack: case depth, IT FAQ, honest scope — ammunition for internal sell.
